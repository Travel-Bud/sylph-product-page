# Explore: six directions for the landing (2026-09-22)

Built on the local branch `explore/landing-directions-2026-09-22` (cut from `landing-two-sides` at `cb87be9`) and
shipped at `/lab` (index at https://sylph-product.com/lab). Every route under `/lab` is noindex and nothing on the
site links to it. `/` is untouched. Each folder here holds a direction's NOTES.md and a `captures-sheet.jpg` of its
captures (first screen and key moments at 1440 and 390, plus reduced motion); the full-size frames stay on the
local branch. All six pass `scripts/explore-check.mjs` (no console errors, no failed requests, no horizontal
overflow at 1440, 390 and reduced motion) on both the dev server and the production build. Image spend for the run
was about $2.27 of the $15. Book a demo in every direction reads `DEMO` from `site/anchors.ts` (Calendly).

## Departures

**The receipt** (`/lab/receipt`). The page is the Sushi Kanda receipt. A print head sits at the bottom of the
screen, so scrolling feeds the paper and the receipt narrates its own month: texted, matched, read against M-041,
noted, one of five in Dana's queue, approved, filed on line 3, exported. It could beat the current page because
it is the most memorable thing in the set: "Nobody had to chase me." is a headline people repeat. The receipt's
voice lets plain product truth read as charm, and the page costs almost nothing to maintain. To ship it needs a voice
pass, a layout pass between 700 and 1100px, and an OG image.

**Compiled** (`/lab/compiler`). The page is a policy editor. Drag the dinner cap from $75 to $85 and a sample
week recompiles; Sushi Kanda flips to Cleared, citing line 4.1, and the dock shows the draft against the approved
ruleset until someone approves it. It could beat the current page for the controller who distrusts AI: determinism
and citation are shown by the visitor's own hand. It leads with the mechanism, though, against the outcome-first
voice. To ship it, the rule IDs and grammar need aligning with the real engine, and one claim needs a decision:
re-checking a past week against a draft implies a policy preview feature. If Sylph has none, label the week
illustrative.

**The pile** (`/lab/pile`). A data story in the manner of interactive journalism. The first screen is the
98-charge sample month as a heap of slips under "You review the exceptions, not the pile." Scrolling sorts it:
receipts fan in by text, email and upload, currencies convert, duplicates are ringed, the rules sweep row by row
with thresholds drawn, 84 file into the report, and the 14 left are named with their citations. A counter falls from
98 to 14. It could beat the current page because it is the only page that shows the outcome at the scale a
finance lead actually lives with; every earlier version showed one charge. To ship it, the story should be driven
by real engine output, the marks need keyboard focus, and the text window at 390 needs more room.

## Two sides, pushed

**Two faces** (`/lab/janus`). The page is split down the middle for its whole length: Priya's paper half and
Dana's ink half slide against each other and line up only at each shared moment. The headline and clock are cut
across the seam, and at month end the report's halves meet and a green ground opens over both. It could beat the
current page because the two-sides idea becomes the structure itself rather than alternating scenes, and the first
screen is the strongest single image in the set. The Janus name comes for free. On a phone the seam turns
horizontal, which works but loses some of the magic. To ship it needs real iPhone Safari testing and a copy review.

**Drawn** (`/lab/comic`). Priya and Dana as the leads of a nine-panel graphic novel set in five new clay scenes:
a lit Sushi Kanda on a dark Denver street, the counter, the cab, Dana's desk the next morning, month end. The
product appears only as screens inside the panels. It could beat the current page because the cast finally lives
somewhere, and the warmth is real. It is also the most Awwwards-shaped entry. The cost is imagery: Dana's office
already drifts between two scenes, and every copy change touches art. To ship it needs an art consistency pass and
an OG image.

**Same path** (`/lab/drop`). A playable rules board. Drop one of Priya's receipts through six gates and it lands in
Cleared, Needs a note or Blocked; drop it again and it rides a ghost of its last path and is stamped "Same path";
pour in the team's week and only the exceptions reach Dana. It could beat the current page because determinism
stops being a sentence and becomes something a visitor proves by playing. The machine reads as abstract in stills,
and the board carries the page more than the story does. To ship it needs a layout pass at 641 to 980px and an ear
check of the gate sounds.

## Where I'd put the money

**The pile.** The page exists to sell one outcome, that you review the exceptions, not the pile. The pile is the
only direction that shows it at a month's scale, 98 down to 14, each one named with its rule. A buyer can check it
the way they check a close. Its medium also reads as rigour, which is what "deterministic, cites the rule,
audit-grade" is asking the buyer to believe. It carries no imagery to keep on model. It can absorb the best moments
of the others as sections: the compiler's cap drag as the "rules" step, and the drop's same-path replay as the
determinism beat. Its weakness is warmth. Priya and the rest of the team are already named in the data, so the fix
is to let them surface at the end rather than to add characters.
