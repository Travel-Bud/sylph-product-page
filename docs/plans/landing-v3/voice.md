# Sylph voice for the marketing surface

Distilled from Ben's own words in the YC application (`Janus/yc/application/application.md`). Beliefs, not claims:
the claims guardrails in the landing memory still apply to anything stated as a fact about the product.

## What Sylph believes

- **Policy should be code.** A company writes its spend policy once, in plain language. Sylph compiles it into rules.
  Nobody should have to remember the policy, because the system holds it.
- **Enforcement happens on every charge, not after the money is gone.** The pain buyers name first is enforcement
  that only happens after the spend. A verdict at month end is an autopsy.
- **A verdict must cite its source.** Every decision traces to the policy line that produced it: the rule, the
  threshold, the amount. If it cannot be cited, it is an opinion.
- **AI at compile time, never in the decision.** The reader drafts rules; a person approves them; the enforcement is
  deterministic and repeatable. That is what an auditor is held to, so it is what we are held to.
- **Your cards, your banks.** Institutions cannot switch card programs. Control should ride on the cards a company
  already issues, not on a card the vendor wants to sell.
- **Easy is not enough.** The easiest expense app in the category was given away for free. Sylph is easy where it
  should be and strict where it must be.
- **The audit trail is built in, not reconstructed later.**

## How it sounds

Declarative. Plain words a CFO would say out loud. Short sentences with a verb. Contrast is the native structure
("not X, Y" / "X once, Y every time"). Confident without adjectives: no "powerful", "seamless", "effortless",
"intelligent". Numbers only where they are sample data with a chip. No em or en dashes, no middots. Never a question
as a headline. Compile is our word; use it.

## Ben's direction, 2026-09-01 evening: outcome first

The page speaks to the outcome before the mechanism. Month end is handled; nobody chases receipts; the report is
already there. The mechanism (rules, verdicts, citations) is the proof underneath, not the opening line.

Headlines in that register
- Nothing to chase at month end.
- Close the month without chasing a receipt.
- Your policy does the chasing.

Lede to pair with any of them
- Sylph turns your policy into rules and checks every charge as it happens. Receipts find their own charges. At
  month end the report is already there. You review the exceptions, not the pile.

## Lines in the mechanism register (used on the lab prototypes)

Headlines
- Write the policy once. Sylph enforces it on every charge.
- Your policy, compiled.
- Every charge gets a verdict. Every verdict cites the rule.
- Control on the cards you already carry.

Section heads
- Month end is too late to find out.
- A verdict that cannot cite its rule is an opinion.
- The policy is a PDF nobody reads. We read it.
- Exceptions come to you. Everything else files itself.
- The card stays. The chaos goes.

Lede / support
- Sylph reads your travel and expense policy and drafts the rules. A person approves them. From then on every card
  charge, receipt and booking is checked against the same rules, and every verdict names the rule, the threshold and
  the amount.
- Works with the cards and banks you already use. Nothing to switch.

Roadmap (in build, not yet available)
- Next, the policy rides on the card.

## Off limits (see the landing memory)

Live or AI-chat claims, fake logos, testimonials, metrics, savings percentages, competitor names, "first/only/#1",
SOC2/GDPR badges, "tamper-evident", setup times shorter than same-day, anything implying a Visa partnership,
"Blocked" meaning a declined card.
